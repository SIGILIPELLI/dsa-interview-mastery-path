# Level 4 · Master <span class="level-badge">The interview itself</span>

Knowing algorithms is necessary but not sufficient. In a live interview you are also
being evaluated on how you clarify a vague problem, how you communicate while thinking,
how you test your own code, how you react to hints, and — in separate rounds — how you
describe your past work. Level 4 is about that performance layer, plus the logistics
around it: online assessments, preparing a study schedule, and handling an offer.

A note on honesty: hiring processes vary widely between companies, teams and countries,
and they change over time. Nothing here claims that any particular company asks
particular questions or uses a particular rubric. The lessons describe common *formats*
and the reasoning behind good behaviour in each, so you can adapt to whatever process you
actually face. When in doubt, ask your recruiter what the rounds are — that is normal and
expected.

## What you need before starting

- Levels 1 and 2 at least. Level 3 helps but is not required to benefit from this level.
- A way to practise out loud: a friend, a peer-mock partner, or simply recording
  yourself. Much of this level only works if you actually speak.
